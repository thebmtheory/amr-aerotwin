In 2026 Aston Martin loses time on the straights at about twice the rate of any other team.

Use the bar at the top to pick the season, the Aston Martin drivers and the tracks. Every section says which of those choices it follows.

Where the gap comes from

Each team’s best lap is aligned with the pole lap and the time difference is split into corners (braking, apex and exit, 120 m either side of each marker) and everything in between. Dividing the time lost in each by the time the pole lap spends there gives a loss rate. Points below the diagonal lose less per second on the straights than in the corners; in both seasons almost every team sits well below it.

    Lap simulator, calibrated to the real lap

    A quasi-steady point-mass simulator (grip μ(g + qCLA/m) on a friction ellipse, a power limit and drag) is fitted to the measured speed trace by maximum a posteriori estimation. Uncertainty comes from a Laplace approximation, cross-checked with a Metropolis sampler, with the residual autocorrelation turned into an effective sample size. A Gaussian process on the remaining error then shows where point-mass physics is wrong.
