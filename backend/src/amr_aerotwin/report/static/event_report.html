Method and data

Laps and references

Qualifying: each team's fastest lap that passed every data check, against the fastest clean lap of the session. Gaps in the qualifying order use official lap times for every driver, including laps whose telemetry failed a check.

Laps are projected onto one reference line built from GPS, with the GPS-to-car-data clock offset found per lap. Time comes from the measured timing channel, so section and corner times add up exactly to the official gap.

Sections and corners

Corners are the archive's official turn positions; each takes 120 m either side of its apex, split into braking, apex and exit. Everything else is "straights". Corner classes come from the pole lap's minimum speed: low under 130 km/h, medium 130 to 220 km/h, high above.

Straights and grip

Straights are the pole lap's full-throttle runs of at least 400 m. Every team's best lap is compared at the same positions, so gradient and wind are shared. Grip comes from cornering-limit points fitted against dynamic pressure; it is an effective point-mass figure, not a tyre or aero measurement.

Race pace uses green-flag laps with fuel burn and tyre wear as fixed effects; intervals are a delete-one-stint jackknife.

Limits

Public data has no fuel loads, ERS state, ride heights or tyre temperatures, so straight-line losses cannot be split between drag, engine power and energy strategy. Laps are set at different moments in qualifying, and track evolution mostly adds grip. One lap per team is a single sample; the season-level analysis in the main report has the intervals.
