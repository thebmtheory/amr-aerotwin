# Where Aston Martin loses time: technical note

*Generated from `data/processed/season.json` by `aerotwin report`. Public data only; not affiliated with Aston Martin Aramco F1 Team. Intervals are 90% bootstrap intervals over events unless stated, widened for small samples (expanded percentile bootstrap: with five to ten events plain percentile intervals cover only about 80%).*

## Findings

1. **In 2026 Aston Martin loses time on the straights at about twice the rate of any other team.** Loss rate = time lost to the pole lap in a section ÷ the pole lap's time there, summed over events. Aston Martin's 2026 straight-line rate is 3.97% [3.31, 4.63] against 4.24% in the corners, a ratio of 0.94 [0.71 to 1.31]. Every other team with five or more non-pole events has a straight-line rate between −0.10% and 1.94%; Aston Martin's is 2.05 times the highest of them (Cadillac) [1.56 to 2.75, the maximum over rivals taken inside each bootstrap draw], so "twice" is the central estimate, not a lower bound (54% of bootstrap draws exceed 2). Teams at least 1% off pole over a lap have straights-to-corners ratios between 0.27 and 0.68. Cadillac, as slow over a lap (3.99% vs 4.15%), loses 1.94% on the straights. Nearer the front the ratio is unstable because the corner deficit is close to zero; Ferrari is a front-runner that loses more on the straights (1.06%) than in the corners (0.05%), at a much lower level than Aston Martin. In 2025 Aston Martin's ratio was 0.19 [0.02 to 0.38], in line with the other teams (0.02 to 0.49). Raw seconds by corner type are misleading here: cars spend the most time in slow corners, so slow corners carry the most seconds for almost every team.
2. **Where on the straight.** Compared piece by piece along the main straights (those clear of the timing line), Aston Martin starts 5.2 km/h down, holds that through the first half (−2.8 km/h [−5.1 to −0.3]) and loses 3.4 km/h more in the second half [−5.0 to −1.9], at full throttle, on 8 of 10 main straights at 7 events (leaving out any one event: −3.8 to −2.9). A late loss alone is not unusual: McLaren loses 2.0 km/h in the second half too, the next largest, and several teams show smaller ones in both seasons. What sets Aston Martin apart is the size of both parts: no other team starts the straights more than 4.0 km/h off the field. The shape does not identify the cause: a point-mass simulation reproduces it with extra drag alone, and energy deployment ending earlier or harvesting at full throttle fit it as well. In 2025 the same second-half change was −0.1 km/h [−0.7 to 0.7]. Main straights cost 0.35 s per lap [0.22 to 0.50] in 2026.
3. **Power vs drag is not identifiable from public timing data.** Over one straight the two terms are collinear; fits need at least 200 m of clean straight and a 60 km/h speed range and are reported only where that holds. An earlier version of this project claimed a ~36 kW power deficit; it came from not correcting for gradient and comparing cars on different stretches of track, and is withdrawn.
4. **The teams decide the 2026 order.** Pooled over events, between-team differences (car and driver line-up together) explain 90% [83, 94] of the variance in qualifying gaps in 2026, against 48% [42, 54] in 2025; in the median event, 93% and 47% (laps outside 107% of the best excluded).
5. **Physics accounting.** Giving the pole car's calibrated simulator Aston Martin's measured grip and top-speed gap accounts for 109% of its mean 2026 gap over the 8 events where the geometry check passes and both teams have grip and top-speed measurements, with a wide interval once the grip ratios' uncertainty is included [59, 276%]: the measured deficits are of the right size to explain the gap, not a split of it (event-level R² −0.60). The same accounting for 2025, when the gap averaged 0.97 s against 3.49 s, gives 110% [36, 284%] with event-level R² −1.5 over 18 events. It is an accounting of a large deficit, not a forecast of small ones.
6. **The 2026 Hungary upgrade: suggestive, not established.** A changepoint model not told upgrade dates rates the Hungarian Grand Prix the most likely single shift (posterior 0.31 vs 0.36 for no change; 0.13–0.82 across the priors in the table below). Given a change the step is −1.25% [−2.16 to −0.18]; allowing for no change, −0.80% [−2.03 to 0.00]. A rise and a fall fit better than one step: two changes (+1.4% at the Barcelona Grand Prix, −2.1% at the Hungarian Grand Prix) are 3.6 times more likely than one, so much of the drop undoes a mid-season peak. Fast-corner grip vs the other teams went 92.0% → 98.9% (7 events before, only 4 after). The difference-in-differences test cannot settle it: Aston Martin's own 2026 estimates vary 5.3 times more than its rivals', so the rival placebos are not a fair reference, and against its own 9 other dates no test can go below p = 0.10 (the Hungarian Grand Prix gets 0.10).
7. **Wind is left out, on evidence.** Rotating the wind direction through 360° across 11 windy sessions, no orientation improves the acceleration fit by more than a few percent and the assumed one was best in 9% of them (chance: 8%). Session-level wind readings are not informative enough, so dynamic pressure uses ground speed. Earlier wind claims are withdrawn.
8. **Data note.** Placing car data by GPS position needs a per-lap timing correction in this archive (median 0.11 s, about 9 m at 300 km/h); the archive's odometer distance is unaffected. Frozen car-data stretches (e.g. Baku 2026), car-data stalls (speed, throttle and brake held for 0.5–1.5 s while GPS shows the car braking, then an impossible jump) and the feed's 104% throttle marker are detected and handled.

## Data

Fastest valid qualifying lap of every driver at 37 Grands Prix (2025 and 2026 to date), plus every lap of each race, from the public TracingInsights archive of F1 live timing. Car data (speed, throttle, brake, gear, rpm) arrives at roughly 4–10 Hz and GPS at about 4 Hz. 723 of 760 laps pass the automated data checks. Excluded events and reasons are listed in the report; wet qualifying sessions (Las Vegas Grand Prix 2025) are left out of everything built on qualifying. Gaps are to the fastest lap of qualifying, called the pole lap here; it is the Q3 pole at every event except 2025 Hungary, where a Q2 lap was faster. Regulation figures (minimum mass, MGU-K taper) and Aston Martin's declared upgrades come from FIA documents (data/reference/SOURCES.md).

## Method

1. **Alignment.** Each lap is projected onto the path of the fastest lap that passes every check. The archive linearly interpolates GPS onto car-data timestamps; original fixes are identified (whole-decimetre coordinates) and the offset between the GPS and car-data streams (median 0.11 s) is estimated per lap by matching GPS-derived speed to wheel speed. The archive's lap clock itself is often offset from the timing line, for both streams together (up to 118 m at the line on laps that pass: ALO at the 2026 Austrian Grand Prix), so each lap is re-timed: positions stay where GPS puts them and time is mapped so the car is at the line at 0 and at the official lap time. Checked against the official sector times, which come from separate timing loops, the aligned clock is off by 0.028 s in the median lap (0.041 s RMS, worst 0.14 s).
2. **Geometry.** Curvature is a smoothing spline of x(t), y(t) through raw fixes (time-domain, so position noise does not scale with speed), with the smoothing level chosen per event by the GPS-speed cross-check, then the median across all drivers. Integrated curvature must match the circuit's turn count (one for most circuits, zero for figure-of-eight Suzuka).
3. **Lap simulator.** Quasi-steady point mass: grip μ(g + qC<sub>L</sub>A/m) on a friction ellipse, power limit, drag and rolling resistance, closed-lap forward/backward passes. Calibrated to each measured lap by MAP with weak log-normal priors; Laplace posterior with an AR(1) effective sample size; checked against a Metropolis sampler; a Gaussian process on the residual shows where point-mass physics fails.
4. **Grip.** One near-limit point per corner (flat-out corners excluded) gives a line of limit acceleration against dynamic pressure; it is read only at two event-wide reference pressures because tyre load sensitivity makes absolute μ and C<sub>L</sub>A unidentifiable from public data.
5. **Straights.** Main straights from the pole lap are split into ten equal pieces; each team's speed in each piece is compared with the other teams' median. Joint power and drag fits use the acceleration phase only (up to the field-median peak), gravity removed with GPS altitude, tow-free samples, 2026 speeds below the MGU-K taper.
6. **Corner model.** Minimum corner speed at unseen circuits, cross-validated by whole circuit. Physics baseline: the lap simulator on the held-out track with a nominal car from the training events' calibrations only. Hybrid: gradient boosting on its residual. Split-conformal intervals; exact baseline Shapley values of the reported model.
7. **Race pace.** Fixed effects for driver, compound and per-compound tyre degradation plus a fuel term per lap on green-flag laps; intervals from a delete-one-stint jackknife, checked for coverage of the fuel effect and of team gaps with autocorrelated errors.
8. **Season.** Supertime gaps; per-event one-way random-effects variance split; single-changepoint model with exact normal-inverse-gamma evidence, effect averaged over the changepoint posterior (given a change, and over all models), prior sensitivity in the table above, and a two-change model as a check on a mid-season peak; upgrade difference-in-differences (team and event fixed effects, team-specific track-type slopes) with permutation p-values against rivals and against the team's own other dates, the fairer of the two used as the reference, Holm adjustment and an exact permutation minimum detectable effect.

## Validation

- Automated tests (backend/tests): analytic physics (constant-radius circle, terminal speed, braking distance), parameter recovery and interval coverage on synthetic data, leakage guards, exact Shapley efficiency, JavaScript–Python simulator parity, and an end-to-end run on real Silverstone data.
- Corner model, out of sample by whole circuit: steady-state formula R² 0.65, lap sim on GPS geometry alone R² 0.92, gradient boosting R² 0.83, hybrid R² 0.93 (RMSE 19.2 km/h); within-corner R² ≤ 0 for all models (they do not separate cars at the same corner). Conformal coverage 89.9% at a 90% target (median event 93%, worst 49%).
- Lap-sim accounting of the 2026 gap: 109% of the mean gap [59, 276%], correlation across its 8 events r = 0.62 (Spearman 0.57), event-level R² −0.60; in 2025, R² −1.5. With 8 events and inputs from the same laps this is an accounting, not a forecast.
- Upgrade effects (difference-in-differences, 4 events either side). Each is tested against rivals given the same date (team placebos) and against Aston Martin at its other dates (time placebos); when Aston Martin's own estimates are more than 1.5 times noisier than its rivals', only the time placebo is a fair reference. Detectable = the effect the reference test finds with 80% power at the 10% level. With 5 upgrades tested, no Holm-adjusted p can fall below 0.50, so none can be called significant after correction:
  - 2025 Emilia Romagna Grand Prix: −0.90% (detectable ≥ 0.88%; reference rivals, noise ratio 1.1; p vs rivals 0.10, p vs own dates 0.06, Holm-adjusted 0.50; 4/4 events)
  - 2025 British Grand Prix: −0.02% (detectable ≥ 0.88%; reference rivals, noise ratio 1.1; p vs rivals 1.00, p vs own dates 0.89, Holm-adjusted 1.00; 4/4 events)
  - 2025 Belgian Grand Prix: +0.05% (detectable ≥ 0.88%; reference rivals, noise ratio 1.0; p vs rivals 1.00, p vs own dates 0.89, Holm-adjusted 1.00; 4/4 events)

## Limitations

- Public telemetry has no ride heights, suspension, tyre temperatures, fuel loads or ERS state. Grip and aero figures are effective point-mass values.
- Energy deployment is inferred from speed; the ERS state is not published, so the late-straight loss could also reflect harvesting strategy or top-end drag in active-aero straight mode.
- Laps are set at different times in a session (a car knocked out in Q1 sets its best lap on a greener track than pole). Track evolution mostly adds grip, so it inflates corner loss rates more than straight-line ones.
- Upgrade windows are short (four events) and tracks differ; placebo tests guard against chance but not against every confounder. Overlapping windows (Hungary and Dutch GP 2026) are not independent.
- Mass is the regulation minimum plus an assumed 5 kg of qualifying fuel; a 2 kg error moves C<sub>L</sub>A estimates by about 0.25%.

## What this would look like with team data

With ERS state-of-charge, MGU-K torque and engine maps, the power-equivalent deficit could be split into ICE output, deployment strategy and harvesting losses. With ride-height and aero-map data, the grip-vs-dynamic-pressure line becomes a direct track-to-tunnel correlation check. The calibration and discrepancy framework is built so that higher-fidelity simulation outputs can replace the point-mass model without changing the rest of the pipeline.
